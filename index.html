Before work begins

Verify the existing provision first, then plan around it.

Wiring routes and panels are not always in plain view. A bungalow or split-level with a finished basement may have a new load's cable running through a finished ceiling, and its panel may sit in a utility room, a closet or behind shelving.

Before any new equipment is made to depend on a spare circuit, outlet or rough-in, list what you believe is there and where it ends. Ask the contractor to confirm each item on site, since appearance alone proves little.

How do I find out whether a spare circuit is really usable?

A labelled breaker or a capped cable does not prove a circuit is ready. Photograph what you can see from the outside, then ask the contractor to confirm where it leads and what it can carry.

What papers help when I am relying on older work in the house?

Gather earlier ESA paperwork, inspection reports, invoices and any drawings from a past renovation. Records can be incomplete, so the contractor still checks the installation itself before treating any item as dependable.

Should I buy equipment before the contractor has looked at the site?

It is safer to wait for the assessment. Equipment chosen first can assume a location, a circuit or a clearance that turns out to be unsuitable, and returning it may be difficult.

How can I compare two quotes that rely on the same existing wiring?

Check that both quotes name the same existing items and say whether each was confirmed or assumed. A quote that assumes a spare circuit works leaves a gap the written scope should close.
