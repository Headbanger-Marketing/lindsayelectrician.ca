Electrical decisions belong early in the renovation

A renovation changes how the space will be used and what equipment it must serve. Provide the drawings and appliance specifications before the electrical scope is finalized. A Licensed Electrical Contractor can assess retained wiring, new circuits and the proposed loads. Municipal building permissions and ESA electrical notification are separate matters, and the project may require both.

Prepare the scope for your Lindsay property

Photograph any cable, box or splice you find from a safe distance, and mark its position on a sketch of the room. Note which finished surface was opened and who opened it, without touching or moving the wiring.

Ask the contractor whether the unexpected wiring is live, abandoned or serving a room you have not considered, and who decides whether it stays. Ask what each answer would change in the scope and in the order of trades.

What should the assessment establish?

A wall opened during a renovation in Lindsay shows wiring nobody expected. The contractor traces each cable from its source to its end and names what it feeds. Every run then gets a verdict of keep, remove or replace before the wall is closed. The scope fits this discovery around insulation, drywall and the other trades, and where the plan changes, the ESA notification reflects the revised work. A brief written note of these findings, kept with the renovation papers, helps whoever opens the walls next.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Is unexpected wiring in an opened wall a reason to stop the job?

Usually the work nearby should pause until the contractor has looked at it. Continuing to cut, insulate or cover around unknown cable can make the later assessment harder and the repair more disruptive.

Can old wiring I find be reused in the new layout?

That decision belongs to the contractor after an assessment. Age and appearance alone do not settle it, so keep the photographs and ask for the reuse or replacement decision to be written into the scope.
