A symptom is a starting point, not a diagnosis

Loss of power, flickering, recurring protective-device trips or a damaged device can have different causes. The useful information is what happened, where and when, not a guess made from equipment age. A Licensed Electrical Contractor should assess the installation within a defined diagnostic scope and explain the identified cause before repair work is approved.

Prepare the scope for your Lindsay property

Write down when the power returned, which rooms or devices behaved differently afterwards and what was running beforehand. Note any warm surface, smell or buzzing, and leave the item alone instead of touching or resetting it.

Ask the contractor how the assessment will separate a problem that existed before the interruption from one it caused. Ask whether a basement circuit or a finished-wall location changes how the cause can be traced.

What should the assessment establish?

What changed after the power went off and came back, and where? The report records each symptom against the circuits or devices it touches. Is the contractor looking at the existing installation, a single device or the incoming connection to the Lindsay house? That choice of focus is written down, and the identified cause is named before any repair is approved. How far can the work go without opening finished surfaces? The record states plainly whether the work is limited to what is within reach, and any further steps the contractor recommends are listed apart, each priced separately.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Should I reset a breaker that tripped when the power came back?

Do not keep resetting it. If it trips again, leave it off and tell the contractor which circuit and which devices were involved, so the assessment starts from facts instead of guesses.

Can a device be damaged by the power going off and coming back?

It can be, which is why a device that behaves differently afterwards should be reported. Stop using anything that shows heat, a smell or sparking, and let the contractor assess it before it is used again.
