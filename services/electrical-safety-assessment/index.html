A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. Prepare the scope for your Lindsay property

Collect any seller disclosure, home inspection report, insurer letter and earlier ESA paperwork, and write down how long the home was empty. Photograph visible signs of moisture, pests or damage around the panel and in the basement without touching anything.

Ask the contractor what the assessment will cover, whether any testing is included and which areas cannot be reached. Ask what written result you will receive that an insurer or a lender can read.

What should the assessment establish?

The report names its purpose, such as a purchase, a move-in or an insurer request, and lists each piece of equipment and each area the visit to this vacant Lindsay home covered. Concealed wiring and basement rooms each carry a mark: included or left out. The findings keep what was seen apart from what was out of reach, and the report must not imply that the whole property was examined. Items that need an assessment of their own appear on a separate list before anyone relies on them. Every limit is in plain words.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I use the electrical system as normal after the home sat empty?

That cannot be judged from outside. Equipment that sat unused may have changed condition, so ask the contractor to assess it first and avoid using any item that shows damage, a smell or heat.

Does a safety assessment replace the ESA notification for later work?

No. An assessment records the condition of what was reviewed, while an ESA notification relates to electrical work being carried out. The contractor should explain which one your plans need and when.
